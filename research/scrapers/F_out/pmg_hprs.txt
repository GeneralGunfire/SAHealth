 Question NW4628 to the Minister of Health

    01 August 2025 -
NW4628
Mhlongo, Ms N
        to ask the
        Minister of Health
What plans does his department have to digitise the patient administration system through the use of biometric technology, such as fingerprint identification, to (a) improve patient registration, (b) enable access to medical records across public healthcare facilities and (c) prevent potential abuse such as patients collecting the same medication from multiple institutions?
Source
Link
Reply:
a) Improving patient registration
The Department has rolled out the Health Patient Registration System (HPRS) in 3280 facilities. The HPRS includes functionality for biometric technology to support accurate and secure patient registration, and this functionality has been rolled out in 249 facilities. Biometric verification ensures that each patient is uniquely identified at the point of care and helps prevent duplication of records. Implementation of biometric verification is being undertaken in collaboration with the Department of Home Affairs, enabling real-time identity verification using data from the National Population Register. This integration strengthens the accuracy of patient identification and enhances the reliability of health information systems.
(b) Access to medical records across public healthcare facilities
Through the phased implementation of the electronic medical record (EMR) system for Primary Health Care (PHC), the Department is enabling the creation and access of longitudinal patient records across public health facilities. As the EMR is adopted at facility level, authorised healthcare professionals will be able to access relevant patient information across points of care, subject to proper authentication, including biometric verification. This supports continuity of care and more efficient service delivery.
(c) Prevention of abuse such as multiple collections of the same medication
The EMR system facilitates accurate patient identification at the point of service and enables effective tracking of patient visits and medicine dispensing. This supports the detection and prevention of fraudulent practices, such as patients collecting the same medication from multiple facilities under different identities. Integration with programmes such as the Central Chronic Medicines Dispensing and Distribution (CCMDD) further strengthens medicine management, oversight, and accountability.
All these initiatives are informed by the National Digital Health Strategy and are implemented in accordance with the Constitution, the National Health Act, the Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA), and other applicable legislation and policies.
END.
